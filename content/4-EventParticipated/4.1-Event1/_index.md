# Report on the “Cloud Architect” Event

### General Information
- **Event Name:** Cloud Architect Competition
- **Date:** 20/06/2026
- **Location:** 26th Floor, Bitexco Financial Tower, 02 Hai Trieu Street, Ho Chi Minh City
- **Format:** AWS Ecosystem Knowledge and System Architecture Design Competition (Team-based)
- **Role:** Contestant – Member of team **"Gặp phải thằng liều"**

### Purpose of Participation
The event was not just a theoretical sharing session but a practical arena where teams had to maximize their knowledge of Cloud Computing, AWS services, and System Design skills. Competing directly helped me consolidate all the knowledge I had learned, practice problem-solving under tight time pressure, and optimize my teamwork capabilities.

### Competition Format & Content

#### 1. Participating Teams & Bracket
The competition gathered 8 teams (5 members each). I was part of team number 7 – **Gặp phải thằng liều**.
The Quarterfinal matchups were highly competitive:
- KLKAT vs PrimeOps
- Team newbie vs Young Flame IT
- Loser vs Ngũ Đại Hiệp
- **Gặp phải thằng liều** vs **Lifelong Learners**

#### 2. Question Levels and Scoring Mechanism
The question bank was divided into 3 levels, requiring candidates to combine theoretical knowledge with practical technical requirement analysis:
- **Practitioner Level (10 points/question):** Focused on basic Cloud concepts, Global Infrastructure, IAM security, Pricing, and the AWS Well-Architected Framework.
- **Associate Level (20 points/question):** Required deeper analysis of EC2, S3, VPC, RDS, as well as problems regarding Load Balancing, Auto Scaling, High Availability, and Disaster Recovery.
- **Professional Level (50 points/question):** The most challenging problems regarding Enterprise Architecture, Multi-Account setups, Hybrid Cloud, Migration Strategies, Cost Optimization, and Large-scale Security.

#### 3. Tactical Abilities
The competition became more dramatic as each team was granted 2 special abilities (can only be used once per match), requiring risk management thinking:
- **Minimal Risk:** Used when facing a difficult question with uncertain answers. If wrong, no points are deducted; if right, the team only receives 50% of the points.
- **Star of Hope:** Used for questions the team is extremely confident about. A correct answer doubles the points, but a wrong answer doubles the point deduction.

### Competition Experience & Results

As a member of team "Gặp phải thằng liều", I directly participated in discussing tactics and finalizing answers. Our team's processing workflow for each question was very urgent:
1. Immediately identify the Core Service (Key AWS topic).
2. Analyze the constraints of the problem.
3. Use the process of elimination for illogical answers.
4. Assess the risk to decide whether to deploy a "Special Ability".

The biggest challenge was not just memorizing service names, but being able to explain **"Why is solution A more optimal than solution B?"** based on four pillars: Performance, Security, Availability, and Cost.

**Final Result:** Although our team fought hard, the two most outstanding teams that advanced to the Finals were **KLKAT** and **Ngũ Đại Hiệp** (scheduled to compete on July 11, 2026), while **Young Flame IT** finished in Third place. Nevertheless, all 8 teams had a fantastic learning experience from analyzing the solutions proposed by their opponents.

### What I Learned

#### Hard Skills
- Mastered AWS Infrastructure architecture (Regions, Availability Zones).
- Gained a firm grasp of core services: Compute (EC2), Storage (S3), Networking (VPC), Database (RDS).
- Deepened understanding of High Availability (HA) system design, Auto Scaling, and Elastic Load Balancing (ELB).
- Applied strict security design principles and operational cost optimization.

#### Soft Skills
- Technical Communication: Presenting ideas concisely and clearly to convince teammates within seconds.
- Decision-making under pressure.
- Risk Management and strategic assessment.

### Application to Real-world Project (Cloud Finance Platform)

The challenging problems from the competition provided an excellent foundation for me to apply directly to the design process of my **Cloud Finance Platform – Personal Finance Management System** project:
- **Network Design:** Applied the mindset of clearly separating Public, Application, and Database tiers within a VPC.
- **Security:** Understood how to configure Security Groups to control traffic flow between microservices, while strictly applying the *Least Privilege* principle when writing IAM Policies.
- **Service Selection:** Became more confident in making decisions to utilize the trio of Amazon ECS (Compute), Amazon RDS (Database), and Amazon S3 (Static Storage) to best balance performance and cost.
- **Stability:** Integrated Load Balancing and Auto Scaling to ensure the financial system operates continuously and reliably (High Availability).
